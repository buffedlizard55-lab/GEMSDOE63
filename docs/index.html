Executive summary

This repository builds one thing: a single-band float32 GeoTIFF that can be uploaded to the DOE GEMS Prize Challenge, plus the evidence that says whether it is worth uploading.

The lane is robust selection. The competition rules force one submission to be chosen before the private scores are known, and Phase 2 re-scores that same submission against an expert-expanded label set. So the truth a submission is scored on is not the truth it can be validated on. Rather than pick the emission that wins one holdout draw, this lane scores every candidate emission in an ensemble of plausible label worlds and picks the one with the best mean DTI penalised by the worst-tail average (conditional value-at-risk, Rockafellar & Uryasev 2000), then prunes greedily.

The headline finding

Because known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are masked out of evaluation in both rounds (organizer, thread 11516) and a "new fault" explicitly can be newly mapped geometry of an existing system (organizer, thread 11536), the densest population of scoreable truth pixels is the annulus immediately around the mapped trace network. Published measurement puts USGS trace displacement in north-central Nevada at up to 400 m = 4 px (Hermant et al. 2025). This lane therefore emits a hard ribbon around the catalogue and lets the CVaR selector choose its width and which distance bands survive.

The selector's answer is counter-intuitive and is the main scientific result: widening the ribbon past the first one or two bands makes the score worse, because the metric's true-positive term is a maximum per truth pixel rather than a sum. A displaced trace coincides with exactly one band; every other band pays false-positive penalty for it without earning anything.
