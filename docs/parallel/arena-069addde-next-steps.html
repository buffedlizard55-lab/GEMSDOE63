Next steps, limitations and blockers

What this session delivered

  • A validated, uniqueness-checked GeoTIFF under docs/downloads/.
  • A holdout instrument (hide-and-recover folds, masking-faithful domain, label-world ensemble, exact metric, CVaR selector, greedy prune) with 62 unit tests, every one passing.

Ranked work list for the next session

  1. Test hypothesis 1 (strain-rate second invariant). The bands are already cached, the hypothesis is the strongest of the five, and it is independent of catalogue geometry, so it cannot collide with this lane. Expected: hours, not days.
  2. Fit the offset distribution instead of assuming it. The whole ribbon width rests on Hermant et al.'s "up to 400 m" upper bound. Measure the actual distribution by comparing the USGS catalogue against an independent trace set (the sibling repos cache trace_segments_utm11.csv and gdr_qfaults_traces.csv). A measured distribution replaces an assumption that currently drives the result.
  3. Add a physics-corroborated prior inside the band. The band says where; nothing yet says which part of the band. Multiplying by a corroboration field raises per-dot credit without widening, which is the one lever left that does not pay more false-positive penalty.
  4. Resolve the depth-to-magnetic-source band. Hypothesis 3 is blocked on whether the feature actually exists in the stack. Read the 19 band descriptions and settle it before proposing the hypothesis as viable.
  5. Ask the forum about labels.tif vs existing_faults.tif. They are byte-identical here. Either the download is wrong or the distinction is nominal, and several lanes assume otherwise.
  6. Pixel-level greedy pruning on the winning band. Band-level pruning is a tractable approximation. A single pass of pixel-level pruning on the surviving band only is affordable and may recover a little more.

What would make this a top-of-leaderboard programme rather than one artifact

The metric rewards coverage at low false-positive cost, and the arithmetic says a dot needs only a 0.0493 chance of landing within 300 m of a hidden fault to raise the score. The binding constraint is therefore not the emission policy — that is now close to optimal for a given prior — but the quality of the prior. Every point of improvement from here comes from a better belief field, not from a better selector. That is the argument for spending the next sessions on hypotheses 1-3 rather than on more selection machinery.
