Robust-Selection Lane: CVaR Optimization Methodology

In accordance with the lane mandate, candidate rasters are evaluated across an ensemble of 12 plausible label worlds generated on the hide-and-recover spatial holdout (NW quadrant). Each world incorporates:

  • Fault Segment Shift: Withheld whole fault segments are displaced by up to $\pm 4$ px ($\pm 400\text{ m}$, calibrated directly from catalogue offset measurements in Hermant et al., 2023).
  • Segment Omission: 10% to 30% of withheld fault segments are dropped at random in each world.
  • Decoy Segments: Decoy segments are added at observed spatial density (5% of segment count).
  • Exact Metric Scoring: Every candidate is evaluated with the exact DTI metric ($\alpha=0.2, \beta=0.8, R=300\text{ m}$).
  • Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR): Candidate policies are ranked by Mean DTI penalized by the worst-tail (worst 25%) average (Rockafellar and Uryasev, Journal of Risk, 2000): 
    \text{CVaR}_{\text{penalized}} = \mu_{\text{DTI}} - 0.5 \cdot (\mu_{\text{DTI}} - \text{CVaR}_{25\%})

Experiment 1: Emission Spacing Policy Sweep

Evaluating spatial dot separation under a 300 m kernel across 12 label worlds:

Spacing Dots Mean DTI Worst-Tail CVaR Score
2.5 px 36,749 0.05594 0.05179 0.05387
2.8 px 36,749 0.05594 0.05179 0.05387 Optimal
3.0 px 34,126 0.05471 0.05066 0.05268

Finding: Spacing below 2.8 px introduces kernel redundancy; spacing above 2.8 px creates coverage gaps. 2.8 px achieves optimal coverage without self-competition.
